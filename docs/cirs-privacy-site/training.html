Staff Privacy Training

This page turns the CIRS staff privacy training outline into a readable training reference for admins, dispatchers, responders, reviewers, and technical operators.

Audience Operational and technical staff

The material is written for people who handle incidents, accounts, infrastructure, and oversight decisions in day-to-day work.

Training goal Operational privacy discipline

The objective is not abstract awareness alone; it is to make staff follow correct routing, access, escalation, and evidence practices.

Evidence expectation Completion must be recorded

Attendance, material version, acknowledgement, and follow-up actions are part of the control, not optional training admin work.

Training Overview

The goal of this material is to make privacy obligations operational for LGU users, not only legal or technical. Staff should leave training able to recognize privacy-sensitive situations, use the platform correctly, and escalate issues without improvising unsafe workarounds.

Training title CIRS Privacy and Security Awareness Training
Audience Responders, dispatchers, admins, reviewers, technical operators
Trainer DPO Office and City ICT training lead, sample baseline
Review date 2026-04-29

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why CIRS processes personal data and which data types are most sensitive
  • Follow minimum-necessary access and handling rules
  • Recognize a data-subject request and route it correctly
  • Recognize a privacy incident and escalate it immediately
  • Avoid unsafe sharing, downloading, exporting, or discussing incident data

Core Modules

Module A

Privacy Basics

  • Data Privacy Act of 2012 basics
  • Transparency, necessity, and proportionality
  • Roles of the LGU, staff, and DPO

Module B

CIRS Data Handling

  • What data the platform collects
  • Why incident narratives, locations, attachments, and identities are sensitive
  • What is allowed in the system and what must not be copied outside it

Module C

Access And Account Discipline

  • Role-based access and jurisdiction scope
  • MFA and password hygiene
  • Shared-account prohibition
  • Offboarding and role-change expectations

Module D

Rights Requests

  • What a data-subject request looks like
  • Where to route it
  • Why staff should not improvise responses outside the workflow

Module E

Privacy Incidents

  • What counts as a privacy incident
  • Immediate escalation expectations
  • Evidence preservation and no-cover-up rule

Module F

Safe Daily Practice

  • Screen privacy
  • Device and browser hygiene
  • Export discipline
  • Messaging and email caution
  • Phishing and social engineering awareness

Module Matrix

Module Main focus Expected staff outcome
Privacy Basics DPA principles, transparency, necessity, proportionality Staff can explain why privacy rules apply to CIRS operations
CIRS Data Handling Sensitive narratives, locations, attachments, identities Staff know what must stay inside approved workflows
Access And Account Discipline Role scope, MFA, shared-account prohibition, offboarding Staff understand least-privilege and account hygiene rules
Rights Requests Recognition, routing, and no improvised responses Staff escalate requests through the documented workflow
Privacy Incidents Trigger conditions, escalation, evidence preservation Staff report incidents immediately and avoid cover-up behavior
Safe Daily Practice Screen privacy, device hygiene, export discipline, phishing awareness Staff reduce common day-to-day disclosure and compromise risks

Role-Specific Add-Ons

Responders And Dispatchers

  • Handling sensitive field information during active response
  • Avoiding unnecessary disclosure over radio, chat, or screenshots
  • Using only the assigned jurisdiction scope

Admins And Supervisors

  • Account issuance and revocation
  • Rights-request and privacy-incident workflow oversight
  • Audit and review expectations

Technical Operators

  • Secret handling
  • Production environment safeguards
  • Vendor and residency change review
  • Backup, restore, and log protection

Role Matrix

Role group Training emphasis Main operational risk
Responders and dispatchers Minimum necessary access, field communications, no off-platform sharing Over-disclosure during active response or screenshot-driven leakage
Admins and supervisors Account issuance, workflow oversight, audit expectations Overbroad privileges or undocumented rights and incident decisions
Technical operators Secret handling, env validation, vendor and backup review Unsafe deployment changes, weak secrets, or unreviewed processors
DPO and reviewers Lawful basis, incident assessment, records quality Poor decision trails or incomplete privacy governance evidence

Suggested Session Format

10 minutes Privacy and platform overview
15 minutes Platform-specific handling rules
10 minutes Rights requests and privacy incidents
10 minutes Role-specific expectations
5 minutes Acknowledgement or short quiz

Evidence Of Completion

Keep these records after every session:

  • Attendance list
  • Material version used
  • Delivery date
  • Quiz or acknowledgement result
  • Follow-up actions if gaps were identified

Evidence Table

Evidence item Why it matters Typical owner
Attendance list Shows who completed training before live access or refresher deadlines Training lead or HR support
Material version Proves which policy and process baseline was taught DPO office or training owner
Quiz or acknowledgement result Provides basic assurance that staff understood the required controls Training lead
Follow-up action log Shows gaps were corrected instead of only recorded Operations lead or system owner

Refresher Triggers

  • At least annually
  • Before live access is granted
  • After a major workflow or policy change
  • After a significant privacy or security incident

Training is not complete when slides exist. It is complete when attendance, acknowledgement, and follow-up evidence are retained.
